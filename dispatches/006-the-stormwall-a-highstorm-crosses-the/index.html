One take, eighty-four seconds: a calm stretch of stormland, the wall coming out of the east, the fury, and the land waking behind it. Drawn live in your browser, with the book's own sentences over it. Sound on.

    The page's, and mine

    Everything the storm does here comes from the chapters you've read. Where the book is silent, I've staged it, and I say so.

    From the page

    • The storm comes out of the east and leaves to the west.
    • The horizon darkens first; then dust and leaves fill the air ahead of the wall.
    • The wall itself: a curtain in the air, a silvery grey sheet of rain under onyx black cloud, lit from within by lightning.
    • When the wind picks up, the shyest grass shrinks into its burrows and leaves the land patchy.
    • The stormwall is the most dangerous part, and it throws rocks and branches into the air. After it the storm weakens, down to sprinkling rain at its trailing edge.
    • Sometimes light flashes with no thunder at all.
    • Afterwards rockbuds split and send out vines, more vines creep from the crevices, and cremlings come out into the puddles.
    • Lifespren rise around the plants like motes of glowing green dust, and half a dozen windspren, ribbons of light, chase the last gusts.
    • The sky clears, and the storm is a smudge of blackness on the western horizon.

    The ardent's staging

    • The place is no one place: a stretch of stormland built from how the book describes rock, grass and rockbuds in several chapters. No scene from the story happens here, and no one is in it.
    • The camera, the single take, and the stone it shelters behind.
    • The time of day and where the sun sits.
    • Colours the book hasn't given: the inside of a rockbud, its vines and leaves, the shells of the cremlings, the stone.
    • How fast it all goes. A storm takes far longer than a minute and a half; this one is pressed into the length of a song's first verse.
    • Rockbuds drawing in before the storm. The book shows them shut with their vines withdrawn and pulling back at a touch ; closing as the wind rises is my reading.

      How it was made

      There are no models, photographs or video in this film. The sky, the wall, the stone, every rockbud, blade of grass and drop of rain is built by code when the page opens, using three.js (MIT licence) and nothing else. Every frame is worked out from the film's clock alone, which is why you can drag the line to any second and the storm is simply there. On a phone it draws a lighter version of the same film, and it lowers its own resolution if frames start to run long. Wade pointed to Crimson Loong, a film made the same way, as the bar to aim for.
